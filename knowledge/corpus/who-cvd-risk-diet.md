# Assess total cardiovascular risk, not single numbers

Estimate 10-year risk of a fatal or non-fatal cardiovascular event using age,
sex, smoking status, systolic blood pressure, diabetes status and, where
available, total cholesterol. Treating a single elevated number in isolation
over- or under-treats a large share of patients.

Assess risk in all adults over 40, and earlier where there is diabetes, a family
history of premature cardiovascular disease, obesity, or tobacco use.

# Diet-related risk

Diet is the largest modifiable contributor to cardiovascular mortality in
several Central Asian populations, driven by high sodium intake, low fruit and
vegetable intake, and high consumption of processed meat and refined
carbohydrate.

Counsel specifically and concretely rather than generally: reduce added salt and
salted preserves; increase vegetables and fruit toward 400 g per day; replace
animal fats with vegetable oils; reduce sugary drinks.

# Management by risk band

Below 10% 10-year risk: lifestyle counselling, recheck in 12 months.

10% to below 20%: lifestyle counselling, recheck in 3 to 6 months, treat blood
pressure and glucose to target.

20% or above, or established cardiovascular disease: treat blood pressure to
target, start a statin, and give low-dose aspirin only for secondary prevention
in established disease — not for primary prevention, where bleeding risk
outweighs benefit.

# Tobacco

Ask about tobacco at every visit and offer brief cessation advice. Cessation
lowers cardiovascular risk faster than any drug available in primary care.
