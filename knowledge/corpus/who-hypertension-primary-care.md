# Diagnosis

Diagnose hypertension when systolic blood pressure is 140 mmHg or higher, or
diastolic blood pressure is 90 mmHg or higher, on two separate days. Confirm
with repeat measurement rather than treating on a single reading.

Measure with an appropriately sized cuff, after the patient has been seated and
resting for 5 minutes, arm supported at heart level.

# When to start drug treatment

Start pharmacological treatment in adults with confirmed blood pressure of
140/90 mmHg or higher. Start at 130/80 mmHg or higher in adults with existing
cardiovascular disease, diabetes, or chronic kidney disease.

Alongside drug treatment, advise reduced salt intake, increased vegetable and
fruit intake, physical activity, weight reduction where relevant, and cessation
of tobacco use.

# Choice of drug

Use any of these as first-line: a thiazide or thiazide-like diuretic, an ACE
inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker, or a long-acting dihydropyridine
calcium channel blocker.

Prefer combination therapy, ideally as a single-pill combination, as initial
treatment for most adults.

ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers are contraindicated in
pregnancy. In a woman who is or may become pregnant, use an alternative and
refer for specialist advice.

# Target and follow-up

Target below 140/90 mmHg in all patients, and below 130/80 mmHg in those with
cardiovascular disease, diabetes or chronic kidney disease where tolerated.

Review monthly after a change of treatment until control is achieved, then at
least every 3 to 6 months.

# Refer urgently

Blood pressure of 180/110 mmHg or higher with symptoms — chest pain, severe
headache, visual change, neurological deficit, breathlessness — is a
hypertensive emergency. Refer immediately; do not attempt rapid reduction in a
primary care setting.
