# Detection

Haemoglobin thresholds defining anaemia: children 6-59 months below 110 g/L;
children 5-11 years below 115 g/L; children 12-14 years and non-pregnant women
below 120 g/L; pregnant women below 110 g/L; men below 130 g/L.

Clinical signs are insensitive but should prompt testing: pallor of the
conjunctiva, palms and nail beds; fatigue and weakness; breathlessness on
exertion; dizziness; brittle nails; and in iron deficiency, unusual cravings
for non-food substances.

# Iron deficiency treatment

Give oral iron: for adults, 60 mg elemental iron daily; for children,
3 mg/kg/day of elemental iron. Take with a source of vitamin C and away from
tea, coffee, milk and calcium supplements, which reduce absorption.

Recheck haemoglobin after 4 weeks. A rise of at least 10 g/L confirms the
diagnosis and the response. Continue treatment for 3 months after haemoglobin
normalises in order to refill iron stores.

# When iron alone is the wrong answer

Investigate rather than simply treating when there is no response to 4 weeks of
iron; when anaemia is severe (haemoglobin below 70 g/L); when there are signs
of bleeding, including black stool or blood in the stool; in a man or a
post-menopausal woman, where gastrointestinal blood loss must be excluded; or
where the mean cell volume is high rather than low, which points to B12 or
folate deficiency instead.

Severe anaemia with breathlessness at rest, chest pain or altered consciousness
requires urgent referral, not an outpatient iron prescription.
