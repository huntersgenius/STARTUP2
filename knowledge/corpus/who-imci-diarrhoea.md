# Assess diarrhoea

Ask: for how long? Is there blood in the stool? Classify every child with
diarrhoea for dehydration, and additionally for persistent diarrhoea if 14 days
or more, and for dysentery if there is blood in the stool.

## Classify dehydration

**Severe dehydration** — two or more of: lethargic or unconscious; sunken eyes;
not able to drink or drinking poorly; skin pinch goes back very slowly (over 2
seconds). This is an emergency. Give fluid for severe dehydration (Plan C) and
refer urgently to hospital.

**Some dehydration** — two or more of: restless or irritable; sunken eyes;
drinks eagerly or thirsty; skin pinch goes back slowly. Give fluid and food for
some dehydration (Plan B) with low-osmolarity ORS under observation for 4 hours.

**No dehydration** — not enough signs to classify as some or severe dehydration.
Give fluid and food to treat diarrhoea at home (Plan A). Advise the caregiver to
return immediately if the child becomes sicker, is not able to drink or
breastfeed, develops a fever, or has blood in the stool.

## Treatment

Give zinc supplementation to every child with diarrhoea: 10 mg daily for infants
under 6 months, 20 mg daily for children 6 months and older, for 10 to 14 days.
Zinc reduces both the duration and the severity of the episode and the
likelihood of recurrence over the following two to three months.

Continue feeding, including breastfeeding, throughout the illness. Do not give
antidiarrhoeal drugs to children — they do not treat dehydration and they mask
deterioration.

Give antibiotics only for dysentery (blood in the stool) or suspected cholera
with severe dehydration. Routine antibiotics for watery diarrhoea are harmful.

## Danger signs requiring immediate referral

Not able to drink or breastfeed; vomits everything; convulsions; lethargic or
unconscious. Any child with a general danger sign needs urgent referral after
pre-referral treatment.
